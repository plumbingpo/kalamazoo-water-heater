A leaking water heater is not automatically a heater at the end of its life. Plenty of leaks come from parts that are cheap and quick to replace: a relief valve that no longer seats, a plastic drain valve that has started to weep, a supply connector with a tired gasket, or a dielectric nipple rusting at the top of the tank. Some puddles are not leaks at all but condensation, especially on a newly filled tank or a gas unit starting up in muggy weather. The one leak that cannot be repaired is water seeping from the steel tank, usually through the bottom or a seam. For Kalamazoo, MI homeowners, the difference between a small repair and a new heater is why diagnosis comes first.

Our diagnosis is methodical. We dry the area, check the relief valve outlet and discharge pipe, inspect the drain valve, and follow the hot and cold lines up to the fittings on top of the tank. On gas units, we check the combustion chamber for water, which can point to condensation or, more seriously, a failing tank. If the relief valve has been discharging, we measure static water pressure and check for thermal expansion, since a closed plumbing system without an expansion tank can spike pressure every time the burner runs. After all of that do we recommend a fix.

The repair depends on what we found. Valve and fitting leaks are the good news: relief valves, drain valves, dielectric nipples, and flex connectors ride on our trucks, and we can often add an expansion tank or pressure-reducing valve the same day if pressure caused the problem. A leak from the tank body is the bad news, because a rusted-through tank is past saving, and we will say so rather than sell a patch. In both cases, we help limit future damage with a properly drained pan, a leak alarm or automatic shutoff, and a quick lesson on your shutoff valve. How to Tell Something Is Wrong

Most tanks announce trouble weeks or months ahead with rumbling noises, rusty water, or hot showers that keep getting shorter. If any of the following sound familiar, it's worth having the unit checked:

  • Mineral buildup or rust around the hot and cold connections
  • Water stains, soft flooring, or musty smells near the heater closet
  • Water inside the burner compartment of a gas heater
  • A drain valve at the bottom of the tank that drips even when closed
  • Moisture or mineral crust at the end of the relief valve pipe
  • Brown hot water along with moisture around the tank bottom
  • Higher water use with no obvious explanation
  • A wet floor near the water heater that keeps coming back

Act Before It Gets Worse

Left alone, even minor symptoms like rumbling or temperatures that swing from hot to warm tend to mean the heater is working its way toward a breakdown. If you notice any of these signs, contact Kalamazoo Water Heater at (269) 214-9869 for a prompt inspection.

Why This Job Calls for a Pro

Leaks look simple and often are not. Water can start at a top fitting, run down under the jacket, and show up at the base, and condensation on cold tanks add confusion. A wrong part is not the only risk. Plugging a dripping relief valve removes the protection against overheating and overpressure, forcing a corroded connection can split it open, and electric heaters add 240-volt power to the mix. And if the tank itself is failing, no DIY fix will hold for long.

We work through leaks step by step and fix the cause along with the drip. We start by drying the unit, let new water reveal the source, put a gauge on the system whenever the relief valve is involved, and check for thermal expansion problems that make valves weep. We replace valves and fittings with quality parts, swap plastic drain valves for brass, and add an expansion tank or pressure-reducing valve when pressure is the root problem. When the tank is the problem, we say so directly and explain replacement options, including the drain pan and leak protection that can keep the next failure from soaking your floors.

Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Inspecting the anode rod periodically is one of the best ways to delay a tank leak
  • A battery leak alarm beside the heater costs little and can catch a problem days sooner
  • Draining sediment every year helps the bottom of the tank last longer
  • Install a drain pan piped to a safe spot, which matters most for attic, closet, and upstairs heaters
  • A quick monthly look at the base, valves, and top connections catches leaks early
  • Make sure a closed plumbing system has a working expansion tank to absorb heated water
  • High street pressure is hard on water heaters, so a pressure test and regulator can prevent repeat drips
