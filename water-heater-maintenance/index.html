Most water heaters do not simply wear out; they rust out or scale up, and both problems can be held off for years. As water heats, dissolved minerals come out of the water and settle into a layer of sediment on the tank floor. On a gas unit, that layer insulates the water from the burner, so the steel runs hotter and the tank starts to rumble and pop. On an electric unit, it builds up around the lower element until the element burns out. Meanwhile the anode rod, a metal rod designed to corrode so the tank does not, is slowly used up. Once it is gone, the tank itself becomes the target. In Kalamazoo, MI, we see this pattern every week, and most of it was preventable.

Each visit follows the same thorough checklist. The tank is drained and flushed to clear the sediment layer. The anode rod comes out for inspection and is swapped when little of it remains. The T&P relief valve is tested so we know it will open under pressure and close again cleanly. Gas heaters get a look at the burner, pilot assembly, thermocouple or flame sensor, flue, and draft. Electric heaters get element and thermostat testing and a check of the wiring connections. We also measure the air charge in the expansion tank, check the water pressure, set the thermostat to a scald-safe level unless a mixing valve allows higher storage, and inspect the drain valve, connections, and pan.

Tankless systems are included. In place of a sediment flush, they need periodic descaling, since hard water leaves mineral deposits in the heat exchanger that cut flow and efficiency. We isolate the unit, circulate a descaling solution through it, clean the inlet filter, check the vent and condensate line, and look at the error history for patterns. Whatever type you have, you receive a plain summary of the unit's condition and any parts approaching the end of their life. To schedule a visit in Kalamazoo, call (269) 214-9869.

When to Call for Water Heater Maintenance

Most tanks announce trouble weeks or months ahead with rumbling noises, rusty water, or hot showers that keep getting shorter. If any of the following sound familiar, it's worth having the unit checked:

  • Shorter showers before the water turns lukewarm
  • Rumbling, popping, or crackling from the tank as it heats
  • Rising utility bills that point to lost efficiency
  • A tankless unit showing error codes or fluctuating temperatures
  • Moisture, drips, or rust around the base or fittings
  • More than a year since anyone flushed the tank
  • Rusty or discolored hot water at the tap
  • Hot water with a sulfur odor

Why Hire a Professional for Water Heater Maintenance?

The homeowner's side of maintenance is short: keep the area clear, watch for drips, and pay attention to new sounds. The rest is harder than online videos make it seem. Flushing involves scalding water and an old plastic drain valve that may crack or refuse to seal. Anode rods are often seized in place and need significant force, sometimes with a flexible rod for low-clearance spaces. An older T&P valve may not reseat once tested, which leaves you with a drip and a replacement to arrange. And on gas heaters, the burner and vent affect how combustion gases leave the house, which is not a place to guess.

Our trucks are stocked for the usual surprises: a drain valve that will not reseal, an anode rod that has turned to wire, a relief valve that drips after testing. We carry replacements, along with meters for element and thermostat testing and a descaling kit for tankless units. More than parts, we bring a trained eye. How to Prevent Water Heater Problems

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Descale tankless units once a year, or more often in hard water
  • Ask about water treatment if flushing turns up heavy mineral buildup
  • Schedule a flush every year, or every six months if you have hard water
  • Keep the thermostat near 120 degrees to limit scalding and slow sediment buildup
  • Have the anode rod checked every few years and replaced when it is mostly gone
  • Leave open space around the tank, especially near a gas burner
  • Check the drain pan and fittings every few months for drips or rust
