Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Battle Creek

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, test the relief valve once a year to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which limits scalding risk and cuts down on scale. Tankless owners should plan on descaling every year in hard-water areas.

The age of your heater matters just as much. With regular service, a standard tank usually runs 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts acting up, like discolored hot water, popping noises, or moisture around the base, it's smart to start planning a replacement. A planned replacement is cheaper and causes far less stress than scrambling for a new heater after a leak.

Left alone, a small problem tends to grow. Consider a spent anode rod: replacing it is a quick, inexpensive repair, but once it's gone, the water begins corroding the tank steel, and just like that a minor fix becomes a full replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to save our number in their phone and reach out at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves money and spares you a flooded utility room.
